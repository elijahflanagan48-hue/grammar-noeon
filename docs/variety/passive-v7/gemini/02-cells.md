## D1. Tense of the passive

**What is trained:** the learner knows the action is done *to* the subject and chooses the form of `be` (8 tenses: Present/Past Simple, Present/Past Continuous, Present/Past Perfect, Future Simple, Future Perfect).

**How to make the decision non-mechanical:**
- The tense comes from the situation, not from a marker. Examples of logical cues: the result is visible now → Present Perfect ("Look, the fence…"); a process is going on right now as we speak → Present Continuous; the background of another past event → Past Continuous; earlier than another past moment → Past Perfect; an official plan or announcement → Future Simple; a deadline → Future Perfect.
- At least 30% of the tasks are about people: "I was told…", "We're being watched", "Have you been invited?", "She'd been promised…".
- At least 25% are negatives and questions.
- Mix the tenses evenly; no tense more than 20% of the batch.

**Typical mistakes of Russian speakers (for mc/fix):** `is build` / `was build` (no V3); `has been build`; `is repairing` instead of `is being repaired`; `was being` without V3; `will be finish`; Past Simple instead of Past Perfect in a sequence; `were` / `was` agreement with collective nouns and `news`, `police`.

**Examples of the level (do not copy their storylines):**
- `gap` basic: *— Why is Kai so quiet? — He (not / to pick) for the team, and he'd been training all summer.* → **wasn't picked / hasn't been picked** (both correct).
- `gap` medium: *I couldn't print the tickets this morning: the printer (to fix) by someone from IT.* → **was being fixed**.
- `mini` advanced: *When the auction house opened the box, they realised the vase (to pack) in yesterday's newspaper. Nobody knows who did it, but since then every parcel (to check) at the door, and a new rule (to introduce) next month.* → **had been packed · has been checked / is checked · will be introduced / is going to be introduced / is being introduced**.

## D2. Active or passive?

**What is trained:** the main decision of the topic — **who performs the action**. The brackets contain only the verb; the answer can be **active or passive**. In every batch **40–50% of the gaps are active**, and the share must not be guessable from the format.

**Sources of active answers (mix them all):**
1. Verbs that have no passive (intransitive): arrive, happen, occur, appear, disappear, die (only for animals/plants or historically, no tragedies), fall, rise, remain, seem, belong, consist of, last, exist, emerge, collapse (about prices or a scheme). Classic mistakes: *was happened, was disappeared, was arrived, is consisted of, was occurred*.
2. Ergative pairs, where the same verb works both ways: the door opened / was opened; sales increased / prices were increased; the vase broke / was broken; the ice melted; the concert started / was started; the engine stopped / was stopped. The context must make it clear whether there was an outside doer.
3. A non-human doer that performs the action itself: The app reminds you…, A falling branch damaged…, The storm closed… (active) ↔ …was closed by the storm (passive) — choose the context so that only one voice fits the information order.
4. Mixed in one text: the same verb appears active and passive (*The museum (to close) at six. In 2020 it (to close) for a whole year.* → **closes · was closed**).
5. Russian indefinite-personal sentences: «Мне сказали» → I was told; «Здесь не курят» → Smoking isn't allowed here; «Его уволили» → He was sacked / got sacked.

**Rules:**
- The context must be unambiguous: if the active voice would turn the sentence into a different but possible statement, the task is unacceptable.
- In `mini`, at least one gap is active and at least one is passive.
- The voice is not guessable from the bracket: do not write `(to be)` or `(by)` inside the brackets.

**Typical mistakes (for mc/fix):** `was happened`, `was disappeared`, `is belonged to`, `The accident was occurred`, `The parcel was arrived`, `She was died in 1890` (historical), `My phone was broken` vs `My phone broke` (both possible, avoid in mc!), `The prices were risen`, `I was explained the rule`.

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` medium: *Nobody knows exactly what (to happen) to the money after it (to transfer) to the charity's account.* → **happened · was transferred / had been transferred**.
- `gap` basic: *— Has your new desk come? — Yes, it (to arrive) this morning, but it (to deliver) to the wrong flat first!* → **arrived / has arrived · was delivered**.
- `fix`: *The concert was started an hour late because the singer's train was delayed.* → *The concert started an hour late…* (if the meaning is that it simply began late).

## D3. Modal passive and going to

**What is trained:** `modal + be + V3` (rules, possibility, advice), `modal + have been + V3` (deduction or criticism about the past), `has to / needs to be + V3`, `be going to be + V3`, `will have to be + V3`.

**Where it comes from naturally:** rules and notices (*Bags must be left…*), instructions (*The lid can be removed…*), deduction from evidence (*It must have been left here by the cleaners*), regret and criticism (*We should have been warned*), plans (*is going to be turned into…*), possibility (*could be seen from…*).

**Rules:**
- The modal is given in the bracket: `(must / to wear)`, `(should / to tell)`. The learner decides: present or perfect modal passive, and whether there is a passive at all.
- At least 35% of the tasks are perfect forms (must/might/could/should/can't have been).
- Not only must/should: can, can't, could, may, might, have to, need to, ought to, will have to, be going to.
- At least 20% negatives (mustn't be, can't have been, shouldn't have been).

**Typical mistakes:** `must be done` → `must done`, `must to be done`; `should have be told`; `can't been seen`; `might has been`; `is going to repaired`; `must be told` instead of `must have been told` about the past.

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` basic: *Phones (must / to switch off) before you go into the exam hall.* → **must be switched off**.
- `gap` medium: *The soup tastes strange. Salt (might / to use) instead of sugar.* → **might have been used**.
- `gap` advanced: *Nobody told us the trip had moved to Thursday, and half the class turned up on Wednesday. We (should / to inform) at least a week ago.* → **should have been informed** (the bracket gives the verb `inform`, so `told` is not accepted).

## D4. Passive infinitive, -ing and participle

**What is trained:** `to be + V3` after want, hope, expect, deserve, need, refuse, ask, would like, is likely; `being + V3` after enjoy, hate, avoid, remember, mind, can't stand, risk, and after prepositions (without, after, before, instead of, by, for); `to have been + V3` after seem, appear, be lucky, claim, pretend; `having been + V3` at the start of a sentence.

**Rules:**
- The learner decides: infinitive or -ing (by the pattern of the governing word) + active or passive + simple or perfect.
- At least 20% of the gaps have an **active** non-finite form (*He hates interrupting people* vs *He hates being interrupted*), so the decision about voice is preserved.
- Mix the governing words: no verb/preposition more than three times per batch.

**Typical mistakes:** (note: after like/hate/love both `to be told` and `being told` are correct — always include both in accepted); `without noticing` instead of `without being noticed`; `seems to be stolen` instead of `seems to have been stolen`; `Being rejected` instead of `Having been rejected` (meaning of precedence).

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` basic: *Nobody likes (to keep) waiting on the phone for an hour.* → **being kept / to be kept**.
- `gap` medium: *He managed to slip out of the meeting without (to notice).* → **being noticed**.
- `gap` advanced: *(To reject) by three publishers, the novel finally came out with a tiny press in Leeds and sold a million copies.* → **Having been rejected / Rejected**.

## D5. It is said that… / is believed to…

**What is trained:** reporting passive in two models: `It is said / thought / believed / reported / expected / known / estimated / understood / claimed that…` and `Subject + is said / thought / believed / reported / expected / known… + to-infinitive`, where the infinitive is chosen by time relation: `to live` / `to be living` / `to have left` / `to have been stolen`. Also past reporting: `was thought to…`, `It was believed that…`.

**Where it comes from:** news, history and archaeology, science, rumours in the city, gossip in an office, sports transfers, nature (animals are believed to…).

**Rules:**
- Most tasks have two brackets: the reporting verb and the main verb: *The ship (to believe) (to sink) …* → **is believed · to have sunk**. Or `(it / to report)` for the it-model.
- Mix the four types of infinitive evenly + past forms of the reporting verb.
- Not only believe/say: think, report, expect, know, consider, estimate, understand, claim, rumour (only *is rumoured to*), suppose.

**Typical mistakes:** `He is said that he lives…`; `It is said to…` with the wrong model; `is believed to steal` instead of `to have been stolen`; `is considered as`; `People are said…` where *It is said* is needed.

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` medium: *Around 2,000 people (to think) (to attend) the protest yesterday, although organisers say the number was far higher.* → **are thought · to have attended**.
- `gap` basic: *(it / to expect) that the new tram line will halve journey times to the airport.* → **It is expected**.
- `gap` advanced: *The portrait (to believe) (to steal) to order for a private collector — it has never appeared on the market.* → **is believed · to have been stolen**.

## D6. Two objects; prepositional and phrasal verbs

**What is trained:**
- verbs with two objects: give, offer, send, show, tell, teach, promise, lend, award, pay, owe, refuse, grant, hand → the person becomes the subject (*I was offered a job*) or the thing does (*A job was offered to me*); the choice depends on what the text continues to talk about;
- the trap: `explain, describe, suggest, announce, introduce, say` do **not** let the person become the subject: *The rules were explained to us* (not *We were explained the rules*);
- prepositional/phrasal: look after, laugh at, talk about, deal with, pay for, listen to, refer to, take care of, run over, call off, put off, bring up, turn down, look into, carry out, set up, throw away, cut off, break into → the preposition or particle **stays**: *has been paid for*, *was laughed at*.

**Rules:**
- At least 40% of the tasks — two objects (including the explain/describe trap in mc/fix);
- at least 40% — prepositional/phrasal, a different verb in each task;
- the context decides who/what is the subject.

**Typical mistakes:** `I was explained`; `The bill has been paid` → loses `for` when "pay for the bill" is meant; `The baby was looked` (no after); `He was laughed`; `A new car was given him`; `was turned down by` → `was turned by down`.

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` basic: *When I got to the hostel, I (to give) a key and a towel, and that was it.* → **was given**.
- `gap` medium: *— Has the electricity bill (to pay) yet, or is it still on the fridge?* → **been paid for**.
- `mc` medium: *The new rules ___ to us on the first day.* → **were explained** (distractors: *explained*, *were explained us*, *have explained*).

## D7. Get-passive and have/get something done

**What is trained:**
- `get + V3` in informal speech: get hurt, caught, lost, stuck, stolen, sacked, promoted, married, dressed, paid, invited, chosen, delayed, damaged, fined; questions and negatives use `do` (*Did anyone get hurt?*);
- `have / get + object + V3`: a service by someone else (*I'm having my eyes tested*), an unpleasant event (*She had her bag stolen*); the tense is carried by have: `have…done / am having…done / had…done / will have…done / have had…done / must have…done`.

**Rules:**
- The brackets give the object and the verb: `(my hair / to cut)`, `(her passport / to steal)`; the learner builds the word order and the form of have.
- At least 30% — a contrast with the active voice in `which` and `mini`: *I cut my hair myself* / *I had my hair cut*.
- The tense of `have` varies, including Continuous and Perfect; at least 20% questions.
- In `accepted_answers`: both have and get where both fit (except "unpleasant event": get is possible but less common — include it if natural).

**Typical mistakes:** `I had cut my hair` (word order); `I did my hair cut`; `I have my car repaired yesterday`; `She had stolen her bag` (meaning: she stole it herself); `Did you get your eyes test?`.

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` basic: *Your fringe looks great! Where did you (it / to cut)?* → **get it cut / have it cut**.
- `gap` medium: *Kofi (his bike / to steal) outside the library, so he walked home in the rain.* → **had his bike stolen / got his bike stolen**.
- `gap` advanced: *The landlord insists that we (the chimney / to sweep) every year, which is odd, since nobody has lit a fire in it for decades.* → **have the chimney swept / get the chimney swept**.

## D8. make, allow, let, see, hear in the passive

**What is trained:** `be made to do` (with **to**!), `be allowed to do` (let has no passive: *we were let* → wrong), `be seen / heard to do` (the whole action) and `be seen / heard doing` (the action in progress), `be not allowed`, `be expected / be asked / be told to do` as close patterns.

**Rules:**
- The brackets give both verbs: `(to make / wait)`, `(to see / leave)`, `(not / to allow / use)`.
- At least 30% — `fix` and `mc` with the typical mistakes.
- Active contrasts: *They made us wait* (active, no to) ↔ *We were made to wait*.

**Typical mistakes:** `were made wait`, `was let to go`, `were let go home` (in the meaning "were allowed"), `is not allowed using`, `was seen to leaving`, `was heard sing`.

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` basic: *Phones (not / to allow) in the changing rooms — there's a sign on the door.* → **are not allowed / aren't allowed**.
- `gap` medium: *At my first job we (to make / wear) a ridiculous orange cap, even in the office.* → **were made to wear**.
- `gap` advanced: *The suspect (to see / climb) over the back fence at about 2 a.m., according to a neighbour who couldn't sleep.* → **was seen climbing / was seen to climb**.

## D9. Traps: state or action, need doing, born, a passive meaning without a passive form

**What is trained:**
- state vs action: *The shop is closed* (state) / *is being closed down* (process) / *was closed in 2019* (event); *The window was broken when we arrived* (state) / *was broken by a ball* (event);
- `need + -ing` = `need to be + V3` (*The grass needs cutting*);
- `be born` — only passive, and only `was/were born`;
- verbs without a passive in the meaning of "have, fit, suit, lack, resemble, cost, weigh": *This jacket doesn't fit me* (not *is not fitted by me*);
- an active form with a passive meaning: *This fabric washes well; The book sells well; The door won't lock*;
- `be + V3` as an adjective: *I'm interested / worried / bored / tired / married / lost*.

**Rules:**
- `mc`, `fix` and `which` are the main formats here (see the batch distribution).
- In `fix`, mistakes of the types *I was born in 2005 → I am born*, *This dress is not suited me*, *The novel is sold well*, *The car needs to clean* (→ *needs cleaning / needs to be cleaned*).

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` basic: *My grandmother (to bear) in a village that no longer appears on any map.* → **was born**.
- `gap` medium: *The car really (to need / clean) — look at the mud on the doors.* → **needs cleaning / needs to be cleaned**.
- `which` medium: situation: *You walk past the bakery on Sunday; the lights are off and there is a sign on the door.* → **The bakery is closed on Sundays.** (distractors: *is being closed*, *was closed by the baker*, *closes now*).

## D10. By, with or no doer

**What is trained:** whether the doer is needed at all (*by someone / by people* is pointless; *by the director* is new information); `by` (doer or cause, including non-human: *by a falling branch*) vs `with` (instrument: *with a knife*) vs the preposition of the governing word (*filled with, covered in/with, made of / from, known for / as / to, crowded with, surprised at / by, interested in, involved in, worried about*).

**Rules:**
- `mc` — choosing the preposition or the most natural sentence (with and without by …);
- `fix` — an unnecessary `by somebody`, `by` instead of `with`, `made from` / `made of`;
- `which` — which version sounds natural in a given genre (news without the doer, a story with an important doer);
- `mini` — 2–3 gaps of the prepositions/forms inside a story.

**Typical mistakes:** `was killed with a lightning` (there should be by), `was cut by a knife` (instrument → with), `This house was built by builders`, `is known by everybody`, `covered by snow` / `covered in snow` (both possible! do not put both in mc), `made from wood` vs `made of wood` (both are used — avoid ambiguity).

**Examples of the level:**
- `mc` basic: *The square was crowded ___ tourists taking photos of the clock.* → **with** (by / of / from).
- `which` medium: *A news website reports a bank robbery in which nobody knows the robbers' names. Which sentence fits best?* → **The bank was robbed shortly after 10 a.m.** (distractors: *…by some people…*, *Somebody was robbing…*, *The bank robbed…*).

## D11. Questions, negatives and word order

**What is trained:** the passive in questions (*When was it built? Who was it written by? What is this made of? How is it pronounced? Has anyone been told?*), with a preposition at the end (*Who was it designed by? What is it used for?*), negatives with adverbs and their position (*has never been seen, will probably be announced, is still being discussed, had already been sold, hasn't been decided yet, can't easily be fixed*).

**Rules:**
- The brackets give the words that must be used in random order: `(this word / to pronounce)`, `(still / not / to find)`.
- At least 50% questions, including indirect ones (*Do you know when it was built?* — word order like a statement!).
- Prepositions at the end of a question — at least 20% of the tasks.

**Typical mistakes:** `When was built it?`, `Who was written it by?`, `Do you know when was it built?`, `has been never seen`, `is being still discussed`, `What is it made from?` vs `of` (with ambiguity — avoid it).

**Examples of the level:**
- `gap` basic: *How (this word / to pronounce) in Scottish English?* → **is this word pronounced**.
- `gap` medium: *The missing painting (still / not / to find), and the police have no leads.* → **has still not been found / still hasn't been found / still has not been found**.
- `gap` advanced: *Do you have any idea who (the first English dictionary / to write) by?* → **the first English dictionary was written**.
